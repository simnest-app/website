Leave an Android phone with your SIM card at home as a gateway. Your iPhone, and your family's, can then answer calls, make calls and send texts on that number from anywhere. Everything is end-to-end encrypted; our servers can't read it.

How it works

No account. Devices pair by QR code, and each one has its own keys.

  1. Install the gateway at homePut the SIM in an Android phone, install the SimNest Gateway and grant what it asks for. A spare phone, plugged in and on Wi-Fi, is ideal.
  2. Pair your iPhoneOpen SimNest on your iPhone and scan the code on the gateway's screen. That iPhone becomes the owner; invite family with a link or QR code.
  3. Head outCalls ring your iPhone, texts arrive on it, and dialling from it calls out on the SIM at home.

Calls go direct whenever they can: same network, public IPv6 on your home Wi-Fi, or a router port mapping. Only when none works does a relay pass the audio along, and it only ever sees encrypted audio.

Our servers can't read your calls or texts

Texts, caller numbers, contacts and call logs are encrypted on the sending device with the receiving device's key. Only that device can open them.

End-to-end encrypted

HPKE (X25519, HKDF-SHA256, AES-128-GCM) with a device signature on every message. Servers and relays only pass ciphertext along.

No account

No name, phone number, location or advertising ID collected. No third-party analytics or ads.

Logs only when you say so

Diagnostic logs are uploaded only when you tap Send, never contain phone numbers or text content, and are deleted after 30 days.

Where things stand

Written down here so you don't find out after installing.

  • The iPhone app is in beta, installed by TestFlight invitation, and not yet on the App Store.
  • The gateway is released on GitHub and Gitee. It works together with the iPhone app.
  • The gateway needs Android 12 or later. Verified: Redmi Note 11 5G (Android 13); Huawei Mate 40 RS works with limits. Other phones are untested.
  • The gateway phone stays plugged in and online. First-time setup turns on Wireless debugging, which the gateway uses to carry call audio.
  • Most dual-SIM phones handle one call at a time: while one SIM is on a call, the other's callers get a busy signal.
  • MMS content isn't forwarded, only a notice that one arrived. For emergency calls, dial from the phone in your hand.

Questions

How is this different from call forwarding?

Forwarding needs another number to answer and can't bring your texts. SimNest uses the SIM at home itself, so people you call still see your own number.

How is this different from eSIM or roaming?

No new SIM, no roaming. The number stays on the physical SIM at home and works wherever you have internet.

Does the phone at home have to stay on?

Yes: plugged in, online, with the gateway running. A spare Android phone dedicated to the job works best.

What's the call quality like?

It depends on the network at both ends. Direct paths have the lowest delay; a relay adds a hop. Your iPhone shows which one a call is using.

Can your servers read my texts?

No. They only pass encrypted content along; the keys stay on your devices.

Why is the gateway open source but not the iPhone app?

The gateway holds the call and text permissions of your SIM, so you should be able to check what it does, and others can help support more phones. The iPhone app and core service are how we keep the product going.

How do I join the iPhone beta?

It's invite-only for now, with limited places. We'll say so here when it opens up.
